Bounty: "Pigeon Pitch, One Line" (donrulezz), slug `u-donrulezz-pigeon-pitch-one-line-af06c6cf`.
Format from the brief: one line, English, at most 200 characters, written to `entry.txt`.
Judge model named in the brief: z-ai/glm-5.3-flash on OpenRouter (house call recorded as superbot, reasoning effort high), pairwise, no ties.

## Scoring criteria (in the brief's exact order, 0–5 each, total /25)

1. **C1 On subject** — delivers a one-line elevator pitch from a city pigeon to investors for its startup.
2. **C2 Funnier** — a real joke that lands, not a mild pun or a straight description.
3. **C3 Original** — original and surprising, an idea or twist not met before.
4. **C4 Pigeon voice** — keeps the pigeon's voice and city life in view; could only come from a pigeon.
5. **C5 Tight** — one line in English within 200 characters, every word earning its place.

## Knockout — top 8 head-to-head

Top 8 by total: #1 (24), #3 (23), #24 (23), #2 (22), #6 (22), #10 (22), #15 (22), #16 (22).
Ties at 22 were broken by C2 (funnier) then C3 (originality), per the brief's order.

### Quarter-finals
- #1 vs #16 — **#1 wins.** Both pitch a delivery flock, but #1 stacks three working jokes (pre-seed literally, runs on crumbs, never in traffic) against #16's single traffic gag, and #1 names the investor offer ("Fund the flock").
- #3 vs #10 — **#3 wins.** The extortion-as-seed-round twist is the bigger laugh; #10's crumb map is clever but reads more as a neat product line than a joke that lands.
- #24 vs #6 — **#24 wins.** "We also create the demand" plus the flywheel-with-wings closer is a complete joke engine in two beats; #6's immovable landlord is funny but one-note.
- #2 vs #15 — **#2 wins.** "We are the drones" is the more surprising reframe and the doubled "seed money. Literally" lands harder than #15's equity-for-crumbs line.

### Semi-finals
- #1 vs #3 — **#1 wins.** #3 is very funny, but its "startup" is a protection racket, which weakens C1; #1 is a genuine delivery startup pitch whose jokes (pre-seed, crumbs as fuel) sit inside the business itself, so it wins on the first criterion and holds on humour.
- #24 vs #2 — **#24 wins.** Both are tight and original; #24's self-created demand is the fresher twist, while #2 spends words repeating "seed money" and scores lower on tightness.

### Final
- #1 vs #24 — **#1 wins, 24/25 vs 23/25.** #24 has a superb single joke, but #1 delivers more of the brief at once: a named investor ask, a concrete flock-scale delivery business, pigeon-only assets (flight, rooftops, crumbs), and two independent puns that both land (pre-seed literally; run on crumbs) plus a traffic payoff for the investor. It is the line most likely to beat varied opponents pairwise, not just one style of rival.
